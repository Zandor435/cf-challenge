## zach — "The Commissioner"

- **tone:** roast
- **display_name:** Zach
- **tagline:** Built the whole thing. Picked Marcus Randall.
- **color:** `#ceb888` (Wake Forest gold)
- **silhouette_cues:** backwards Wake Forest gold cap

**backstory:**

Wake Forest, by way of Baton Rouge — went up to play soccer, didn't play soccer,
came back a Demon Deacon with an LSU heart and a spreadsheet problem. He is the
only man in this pool who will build a scoring engine, a projection model, a CI
pipeline, and an AI art department for a bet you could settle with a handshake.
He has done it once already: a World Cup pool over the summer, a different
crew, a scoring system he wrote and then lost under — none of which stopped one
man from finishing the thing convinced the scoring had cost him a win he had
earned. He built this one anyway, and appeals still go to the author.
Twenty years ago he looked at Marcus Randall and called him the future of our
team. Matt Mauck was standing right there. He has never lived it down and he
never will, because he wrote the website that displays it.

## jonathan — "The Rocket Scientist"

- **tone:** roast
- **display_name:** Jonathan
- **tagline:** Builds spacecraft. Cannot pick a wide receiver.
- **color:** `#c30020` (Texas Tech red)
- **silhouette_cues:** visor (Spurrier persona). Eye patch is foreground-only — never at depth.

**backstory:**

Electrical engineer at NASA in Huntsville — the man works on the actual rocket.
He is a genuine space nerd and the smartest person in this pool by a distance
that isn't close. He also once played minor league baseball, a fact preserved for
posterity by a baseball card of an unrelated middle-aged man who looks exactly
like him, which we have used as his official portrait for years and will continue
to use. And then there are his boys. Drake — the one we all fell for, the one
Jonathan claimed before anyone else could, the one who never gave us a thing.
Jonathan Giles, Texas Tech's conference player of the year, who arrived in Baton
Rouge, produced fourteen total yards, and has been Little Jonno ever since. Sevyn
Banks, whose neck gave out on roughly the first snap of the season. Three
blue-chip endorsements, three separate ways to lose. The man can calculate a
launch window to the second and cannot identify a wide receiver who will still be
on the roster in November.

- **running_gag:** Little Jonno. Fourteen yards. Also the baseball card.
- **draft_tendency:** Rigorous, defensible, cursed.
- **fatal_flaw:** His boys. It's a documented pattern at this point.
